5. Product description  
Within the scope of protection, it is also called single -leaf tea. The fresh leaves of 
the tea plants of the small-leaved Dushan variety, middle-leaved Qitoushan variety, and 
the fine clonal cultivars of both are selected and uniquely handmade into melon- seed-
shaped green tea. It is known to be stalk- free, bud- free, decay -free, odor -free and 
perfectly clean with no inclusions, color matters or any non- tea substances. Lu ’an 
Melon Seed Tea is divided into six grades: ultimate, super first, super second, first, 
second and third.

6. A concise definition of the geographic area 
Geographical boundaries: The protection region of Lu'an Melon Seed Tea includes 
Shipodian Town, Shibanchong Township, Dushan Town, Xihekou Township, and 
Qingshan Township in Yu'an District of Lu'an City, Xianghongdian Town, Qingshan 
Town, and Yanzihe Town, Xiangqi Office, Tiantangzhai Town, Gubei Town, 
Zhangchong Township, Youfangdian Township, Changling Township, Huaishuwan 
Township, and Zhangfan Township in Jinzhai County, Foziling Town, Heshidu Town, 
Zhufoan Town , Mozitan Town, Manshuihe Town, Taiyang Township, and Dahuaping 
Town in Huoshan County, Maotanchang Town, Donghekou Town in Jinan District of 
Lu'an City, Xiaotian Town in Shucheng County. Altogether 5 districts and counties, 26 
townships and towns are now under this jurisdiction. The city boundary is between 
115°20′-117°14′ east longitude and 31°01 ′-32°40′ north latitude. The production area 
of Lu ’an Melon Seed Tea is located in the western part of Anhui Province, near the 
northern foot of Dabie Mountain and the eastern edge. The climate lies in the transition 
zone between the northern subtropical and the warm temperate zone.

is inclined at 25-30°. The leaves are sautéed into desired forms with reed, sorghum or

(2) Fresh leaf requirements  
Picking: Before and after mid -April, when the new shoots of tea plants grow to a 
single bud and three leaves, single fresh leaves of the same size and tenderness are 
plucked directly from the tea plants.  
Pulling: When the new shoots of tea plants grows to one bud and three leaves, the 
new shoots are picked, and the leaves are pulled and graded according to the degree of 
tenderness.  
Picking requirements: no pests or infected leaves, no frost -frozen leaves, or 
damaged leaves, no buds and stems taken when harvesting. 
(3) Cooling down. Cool the fresh leaves on bamboo mats, (4- 6 cm thick), for 4- 6 hours. 
Damp leaves require a longer drying time, After cooling, leaves emit grassy aroma and 
evaporate some water and produce natural scent. 
(4) Raw leaf pot. When the pot is heated around 150 °C, about 80g leaves are put inside 
and fried with reed, sorghum or bamboo brooms for about 2 minutes. The leaves 
become soft, green and transparent with fresh aroma and the wat er content of about 
70%. 
(5) Cooked pot. The pot temperature is about 100 °C, and the frying is done while 
patting leaves into melon seed shapes with lighter force on the young leaves and heavier 
force on the old ones. It takes approximately 4 minutes unti l the leaf becomes dark 
green and fragrant, and the water content is about 45%.  
(6) Fuzzy fire. Around 2kg of leaves are dried in bamboo baskets over a charcoal fire 
with the young leaves thinly distributed and the old ones spread more thickly. The 
temperature of top of the drying basket’s is 80 °C ~ 100 °C, and leaves are turned once 
every 2 -3 minutes until they are green and fragrant, with a water content of about 30%. 
(7) Screening. Use bamboo tools to hold the tea leaves and pick out the leaves that a re 
improperly shaped or have an inconsistent color, floating leaves and non-tea inclusions 
to achieve uniformity.  
(8) Gentle fire. The temperature at the top of the basket is about 80 °C. About 2.5 kg of 
leaves per basket are dried slowly until they are fragrant with the water content of about 
15%. 
(9) Spreading. Spread tea leaves in bamboo baskets or dustpans and leave for more than 
72 hours at room temperature until the leaves are fragrant with a water content of about 
15%.  
(10) Strong fire (also called pulling the old fire). It is framed by a chestnut tree charcoal 
with a height of 40 cm to 50 cm. It requires a high fire temperature and a strong fire, 
which is intense and uniform. Each basket is filled with leaves from 12 -14kg, and two 
people lift the basket over a charcoal fire and fry leaves for 1.5 seconds, then stir the 
leaves. Turn over the leaves for 150 times, without stopping in the middle until the 
leaves turn whitish green with an aroma and the water content is less than 6%.The tea 
dust under the baking baskets is removed.

7. Relevance to geographical regions 
Lu’an Melon Seed Tea is a traditional Chinese tea, the best Chinese green tea, and 
one of the top ten famous teas in China. It is protected by its geographical indication, 
national certification trademark and the unique cultural heritage of the People's 
Republic of China. The quality characteristics of Lu ’an Melon Seed Tea are closely 
related to the climate, soil, environment, traditional craftsmanship and other factors 
derived of 26 townships and towns in Lu'an:  
(1) Relationship with geographical features  
The protection region of Lu’ an Melon Seed Tea is the administrative area of 26 
townships and towns in 5 districts and counties of Lu'an City. The city boundary is 
between 115°20 ′-117°14′ east longitude and 31°01 ′-32°40′north latitude. The 
production area of L u’an Melon Seed Tea is located in the western part of Anhui 
Province, near the northern foot of Dabie Mountain and the eastern edge. The landform 
of the production area lies at the northern base of Dabie Mountain. The Xianghongdian 
Reservoir is located wit hin. The area boasts a high altitude, (a max. 804m), rich 
vegetation, green mountains, clear rivers, hazy clouds, high humidity, fertile soil, all of 
which are suitable for the growth of tea trees. Thanks to the unique geographical 
environment, it has crea ted the excellent quality of Lu'an Melon Seed Tea: fresh and 
mellow, fragrant and sweet, with a lingering aftertaste. 
(2) Relationship with climate characteristics  
The climate is in the transition zone between the northern subtropical and the warm 
temperate zone. The climatic conditions are typical of the north subtropical monsoon 
climate with an obvious monsoon, four distinct seasons, abundant rainfall, moderate 
heat and cold, perfect for tea production. The annual average temperature is 15°C, the 
average precipitation is 1200mm, the absolute humidity is 15.6mb, the sunshine hours 
are 1881 hours, and the frost-free period is 220 days. It is blessed with a humid climate, 
low temperature, fertile soil, and frequent cloud cover all the year round. This ensure s 
that Lu’an Melon Seed Tea is unique in color, aroma, taste, and shape. 
(3) Relationship with soil characteristics  
The soil is yellowish brown with a thicker surface humus layer containing a high 
organic matter content with the pH value of 4.8-5.5. The soil of Lu’an Melon Seed Tea 
Plantation Area is very fertile and has good permeability, which is very conducive to 
the absorption of nutrients. This provides a unique growth environment for the Lu’ an 
Melon Seed Tea plants. 
(4) Relationship with human characteristics  
Xu Guangqi, a scientist of the Ming Dynasty, said in his book The Book of 
Agricultural Administration that "the tea of Lu'an State is the best of tea." The Empress 
Dowager Cixi only enjoys 14 liang every month. Liang Shiqiu recorded in the artic le 
Drinking Tea: "Friends from Lu'an gave me a few teas. The leaves are big and green, 
and the drinkers can smell the wilderness." Premier Zhou Enlai and General Ye Ting 
also had emotional connections with Lu’an Melon Seed Tea. Premier Zhou gave the tea
